The Clinical Dilemma

Documenting Pharmaceutical Histories in Nutritional Consults

Dietitians face daily workflow hurdles when trying to decipher commercial drug prescriptions during initial assessments:

🏷️

Proliferation of Local Trade Names

Patients routinely report brand names (e.g. Januvia, Glucophage, Forxiga, Concor, Euthyrox). Identifying the exact active substance immediately is crucial to understanding metabolic health and structuring appropriate meal timing.

🔎

Wasted Consultation Time on Search Engines

Resorting to public web searches during an appointment looks unprofessional, wastes precious consultation minutes, and surfaces consumer-targeted advertising rather than concise clinical facts.

📁

Fragmented Paper Records

Jotting down medications on paper scratchpads causes loss of longitudinal continuity. Murshid creates a structured, digital medication ledger linked directly to the patient's ongoing clinical profile (Patient 360).

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need to Know

Clarifications regarding database scope, clinical liability, and privacy:

❓ How many medications and active substances are indexed?

Murshid includes approximately 10,000 commercial brand medications commonly prescribed across Arab regional and international markets, systematically mapped to over 1,500 scientific Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs).

❓ What is the primary role of the pharmacology module in Murshid?

It serves as an offline digital clinical documentation atlas. It enables dietitians to rapidly identify active ingredients, verify therapeutic classifications, and record the patient's complete pharmaceutical regimen directly into their nutritional chart.

❓ Does the system perform automated drug-nutrient interaction checking?

No. Murshid does NOT function as an automated medical diagnostic tool or autonomous drug interaction decision engine. It provides documentation and reference lookup capabilities; all clinical judgments and dietary adjustments remain solely with the qualified dietitian.

❓ Can the pharmacology atlas be queried completely offline?

Yes, the complete dictionary is stored locally inside the application using the high-performance Isar NoSQL database, delivering sub-millisecond query results with zero internet requirement.

❓ Can dietitians search by either brand name or generic chemical name?

Yes, the search engine supports instant lookup across both commercial brand trade names and international generic names (INN) with built-in phonetic and typographical tolerance.

❓ Does the app support scanning physical prescriptions via camera?

Yes, Murshid features optical character recognition (OCR) that allows practitioners to photograph medication boxes or printed prescriptions to extract medication names and link them immediately to the patient's record.
