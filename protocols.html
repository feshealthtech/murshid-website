General Wellness & Weight Management

8 Protocols

ADA Standard Balanced

50% C | 20% P | 30% F

The recognized reference distribution for healthy weight maintenance, balanced vital energy, and sustainable lifestyle nutrition.

Guideline Reference: ADA Exchange Lists for Meal Planning
Clinical Indications: Healthy adults, weight maintenance, nutritional foundation.
🫒

Mediterranean Diet

48% C | 17% P | 35% F

Emphasizes monounsaturated fatty acids (olive oil), nuts, legumes, whole grains, and polyphenols for cardiovascular longevity.

Guideline Reference: PREDIMED Trial & AHA Guidelines
Clinical Indications: Lipid profile improvement, arterial health, healthy aging.
📉

Low-Carbohydrate

30% C | 30% P | 40% F

Reduces refined starches and sugars to enhance insulin sensitivity and promote lipolysis for clinical weight management.

Guideline Reference: Endocrine Society Clinical Guidelines
Clinical Indications: Clinical weight reduction, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome.
🥑

Classic Ketogenic

5% C | 20% P | 75% F

Strict carbohydrate restriction combined with high healthy fats to induce nutritional ketosis and ketone body utilization.

Guideline Reference: International Ketogenic Clinical Consensus
Clinical Indications: Specialized fat loss programs, neurological stability.
🥩

High-Protein Protocol

40% C | 35% P | 25% F

Elevates daily protein intake (1.6 - 2.2 g/kg) to maximize satiety, boost thermogenesis, and protect lean muscle mass during caloric deficits.

Guideline Reference: ISSN Sports Nutrition & Clinical Protein Position
Clinical Indications: Athletes, strength conditioning, advanced fat loss protocols.
🌱

Balanced Plant-Based / Vegan

55% C | 18% P | 27% F

Comprehensive plant protein pairing ensuring complete amino acid profiles alongside sufficient B12, iron, and calcium fortification.

Guideline Reference: Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics (AND)
Clinical Indications: Plant-based lifestyles, hypercholesterolemia management.

Endocrine & Metabolic Care

6 Protocols

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

45% C | 22% P | 33% F

Consistent carbohydrate distribution across meals, high viscous soluble fiber, and low-glycemic load targeting HbA1c normalization.

Guideline Reference: ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes, 2024
Clinical Indications: T2D, prediabetes, impaired fasting glucose.
🤰

Gestational Diabetes (GDM)

40% C | 25% P | 35% F

Ensures optimal fetal development and healthy maternal gestational weight gain while avoiding morning hyperglycemia spikes.

Guideline Reference: ACOG & ADA Gestational Diabetes Practice Bulletins
Clinical Indications: Pregnant women with GDM or impaired glucose tolerance.
🌸

PCOS & Insulin Resistance

40% C | 25% P | 35% F

Anti-inflammatory, low-glycemic strategy designed to attenuate hyperinsulinemia, reduce ovarian androgen synthesis, and support ovulatory cycles.

Guideline Reference: International Evidence-based Guideline for PCOS
Clinical Indications: Polycystic ovary syndrome, hyperandrogenism, oligomenorrhea.
🧬

Metabolic Syndrome

42% C | 23% P | 35% F

Targeted multi-component intervention reducing visceral adiposity, hypertriglyceridemia, and blood pressure while elevating HDL cholesterol.

Guideline Reference: AHA / NHLBI Scientific Statement on MetSyn
Clinical Indications: Central obesity concurrent with dyslipidemia and dysglycemia.
🫀

Hyperlipidemia & Dyslipidemia

50% C | 20% P | 30% F

Restricts saturated fats to <7% of calories, eliminates trans fats, and increases plant stanols and soluble beta-glucans to reduce LDL-C.

Guideline Reference: NCEP ATP III & ESC/EAS Guidelines for Dyslipidaemias
Clinical Indications: Elevated total cholesterol, high LDL, hypertriglyceridemia.

Gastrointestinal & Colon Health

7 Protocols
🌾

Low-FODMAP Protocol

Phased Elimination & Reintroduction

Systematic restriction of fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols to relieve visceral pain and bloating in IBS.

Guideline Reference: Monash University Low-FODMAP Diet Protocol
Clinical Indications: Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO).
🔥

GERD & Reflux Management

Small Frequent Meals | Low Irritants

Elimination of chemical irritants (caffeine, citrus, mint, chocolate, spicy items) and reduced dietary fat to prevent lower esophageal sphincter relaxation.

Guideline Reference: ACG Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of GERD
Clinical Indications: Gastroesophageal reflux disease, heartburn, hiatal hernia.
🩹

Active IBD (Crohn's / Ulcerative Colitis)

Low-Residue | High-Protein

Tailors insoluble fiber to reduce mechanical friction across inflamed mucosal walls during flare-ups while supplying restorative protein and micronutrients.

Guideline Reference: ESPEN Guidelines on Clinical Nutrition in IBD
Clinical Indications: Active flare-ups, intestinal strictures, malabsorption states.
⏳

Gastroparesis Management

Very Low Fiber | Low Fat | Pureed

Structured small, frequent liquid and pureed meals to facilitate gastric emptying, alleviate postprandial nausea, and prevent phytobezoar formation.

Guideline Reference: ACG Clinical Guideline: Gastroparesis
Clinical Indications: Diabetic gastroparesis, post-surgical delayed gastric emptying.
🩺

Pancreatitis Care

Strict Fat Restriction (<20-30g/day)

Minimizes pancreatic exocrine stimulation through strict lipid limits, medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) integration, and easily digestible proteins.

Guideline Reference: IAP / APA Acute Pancreatitis Clinical Practice Guidelines
Clinical Indications: Acute recovery phase, chronic pancreatitis with steatorrhea.

Renal & Cardiovascular Health

6 Protocols
❤️

DASH Eating Plan

55% C | 18% P | 27% F

Sodium restriction (<1500 - 2300 mg/day) fortified with potassium, magnesium, and calcium to facilitate vasodilation and lower blood pressure.

Guideline Reference: NHLBI DASH Eating Plan, NIH Publication
Clinical Indications: Essential hypertension, stroke prevention, fluid retention.
🫘

CKD Pre-Dialysis (Stages G3-G5)

Restricted Protein: 0.55 - 0.6 g/kg

Delays renal progression and uremic toxicity by strictly rationing dietary protein while monitoring phosphorus, potassium, and sodium levels.

Guideline Reference: KDOQI Clinical Practice Guideline for Nutrition in CKD
Clinical Indications: Non-dialysis chronic kidney disease stages 3 through 5.
🔄

Maintenance Dialysis (HD / PD)

High Protein: 1.2 - 1.4 g/kg

Compensates for dialysate amino acid clearance with elevated biological value protein, alongside rigorous phosphorus, potassium, and fluid management.

Guideline Reference: KDOQI / ESPEN Guidelines on Renal Replacement Therapy
Clinical Indications: Maintenance hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis patients.
🧪

Nephrotic Syndrome

Moderate Protein (0.8 g/kg) | Low Sodium

Controls massive peripheral edema through strict sodium restriction while moderating protein intake to avoid glomerular hyperfiltration injury.

Guideline Reference: KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for Glomerulonephritis
Clinical Indications: Severe proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, anasarca.
🫀

Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)

Fluid & Sodium Restriction (<2000mg)

Minimizes cardiac preload and pulmonary congestion through sodium and fluid limits while providing adequate density to combat cardiac cachexia.

Guideline Reference: ACC/AHA Heart Failure Nutrition Management Guidelines
Clinical Indications: Chronic heart failure, reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).

Allergies & Autoimmune Care

6 Protocols
🌾

Celiac Disease (Gluten-Free)

Strict Gluten Elimination (0 ppm)

Complete exclusion of gluten proteins from wheat, barley, rye, and cross-contaminated oats to allow small bowel villous atrophy recovery.

Guideline Reference: ACG Clinical Guidelines: Management of Celiac Disease
Clinical Indications: Celiac disease, non-celiac gluten sensitivity, dermatitis herpetiformis.
🥛

Lactose Intolerance Protocol

Lactose-Free | Fortified Substitutes

Eliminates disaccharide lactose to avoid bacterial fermentation, cramps, and diarrhea, with calcium and vitamin D fortification.

Guideline Reference: NIH Consensus Statement on Lactose Intolerance
Clinical Indications: Lactase enzyme deficiency, post-viral enteropathy.
🫘

Favism & G6PD Deficiency

Strict Fava Bean & Oxidant Exclusion

Strict avoidance of broad fava beans, vicine/convicine derivatives, and specific dietary pro-oxidants to protect erythrocyte integrity against acute hemolysis.

Guideline Reference: WHO Working Group on G6PD Deficiency
Clinical Indications: Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency (G6PD).
🦋

Hashimoto's Autoimmune (AIP)

Anti-Inflammatory | Trigger Elimination

Phased exclusion of immunogenic compounds (grains, nightshades, legumes) combined with polyphenol-rich foods to attenuate thyroid autoantibody titrations.

Guideline Reference: Autoimmune Protocol (AIP) Clinical Trials
Clinical Indications: Autoimmune thyroiditis, elevated TPO antibodies, systemic inflammation.
🦐

Low-Histamine Protocol

Fresh Foods | Zero Fermentation

Restricts aged, cured, and fermented foods containing biogenic amines to alleviate symptoms in patients with diamine oxidase (DAO) deficiency.

Guideline Reference: European Academy of Allergy & Clinical Immunology (EAACI)
Clinical Indications: Histamine intolerance, mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS).

Pediatric Catch-Up Growth

High Energy & Protein Density

Elevates caloric and bioavailable protein density to trigger growth spurts and realign weight-for-age and height-for-age with WHO growth standards.

Guideline Reference: WHO / UNICEF Guidelines on Child Malnutrition
Clinical Indications: Failure to thrive (FTT), stunting, post-illness nutritional deficits.

Cow's Milk Protein Allergy (CMPA)

Complete Bovine Protein Elimination

Eliminates casein and whey proteins from maternal diet or transitions infants to extensively hydrolyzed formulas, resolving allergic proctocolitis.

Guideline Reference: ESPGHAN Guidelines on Cow's Milk Protein Allergy
Clinical Indications: Infants diagnosed with non-IgE or IgE-mediated milk protein allergy.
🧠

Pediatric Ketogenic (Epilepsy)

Classic 3:1 or 4:1 Lipid Ratio

Calculated lipid-to-(protein+carb) ratio (3:1 or 4:1) generating sustained blood ketone bodies to inhibit refractory seizure activity under collaborative medical care.

Guideline Reference: International Ketogenic Diet Study Group Consensus
Clinical Indications: Drug-resistant pediatric epilepsy, Dravet syndrome, GLUT1 deficiency.
🧬

Pediatric Phenylketonuria (PKU)

Strictly Rations Phenylalanine

Strict dietary phenylalanine budgeting calibrated against child weight and plasma lab values to prevent neurocognitive toxicity.

Guideline Reference: European Guidelines on Phenylketonuria (PKU)
Clinical Indications: Inborn errors of phenylalanine hydroxylase metabolism.
🍭

Pediatric Type 1 Diabetes (T1D)

Advanced Carbohydrate Counting

Matches rapid-acting mealtime insulin boluses with exact meal carb grams to allow normal school activities, sports, and growth without hypoglycemia.

Guideline Reference: ISPAD Clinical Practice Consensus Guidelines
Clinical Indications: Children and adolescents with insulin-dependent diabetes.

Severe Burn Recovery

Counters intense hypermetabolic breakdown, replacing nitrogen losses, trace minerals, and vitamin C to promote successful skin graft healing.

Guideline Reference: ESPEN Guidelines on Clinical Nutrition in Severe Burns

Sarcopenia & Muscle Preservation

Leucine-Rich High Biological Value Protein

Even protein distribution (25 - 30 g/meal) enriched in essential amino acids to stimulate muscle protein synthesis in aging and post-hospitalization recovery.

Guideline Reference: PROT-AGE Study Group Recommendations
Clinical Indications: Age-related muscle wasting, post-illness rehabilitation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About the Protocols Atlas

Everything you need to know about customizing, citing, and using Murshid templates in clinical practice.

❓ What are clinical diet templates and protocols in the Murshid platform?

They are an evidence-based library of over 67 clinical diet templates built upon guidelines from premier medical and dietetic organizations (ADA, ESPEN, KDOQI, NHLBI, WHO). They provide practitioners with structured initial macronutrient splits and exchange distributions, eliminating repetitive manual setup.

❓ Can dietitians modify and customize these templates freely?

Yes, 100%. Templates are flexible starting frameworks, not rigid prescriptions. Practitioners can customize calories, grams of protein, carbs, and fats, exchange portions, meal names, and timings to fit each patient's exact clinical needs.

❓ Do these protocols make autonomous clinical diagnoses or medical decisions?

No. Murshid is an administrative, computational, and documentation assistant, not a Software as a Medical Device (SaMD). The platform contains no automated clinical conflict resolvers or diagnostic engines; all medical judgments remain the sole responsibility of the licensed dietitian.

❓ What scientific guidelines are these templates based on?

Templates reference the American Diabetes Association (ADA), European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (ESPEN), Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI), National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI), and Monash University for Low-FODMAP.

❓ Can practitioners save custom branded templates for their clinic?

Yes. Practitioners can easily save any finalized meal plan as a custom clinic template with specialized clinical notes, meal breakdowns, and favorite local food items for reuse across similar patient profiles.

❓ Does the protocols directory operate offline without an internet connection?

Yes. The complete library of 67+ protocols is embedded locally on your device within our encrypted Isar database, ensuring instant sub-5ms loading speeds even without internet access.
